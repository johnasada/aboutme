J: Personal Brand Sticker

Elements: line, shape, color, texture · Color scheme: complementary (blue + orange)

A bold, hand-sketched J — one of eight thumbnail variations I drew exploring size, weight, and angle — turned into a die-cut brand sticker. The hatched navy fill gives it texture and depth, while the single solid orange dot at the hook acts as the one point of emphasis against an otherwise monochrome mark.

Reflection

I chose this sketch out of my eight thumbnails because the hook shape gave me a natural spot to add a focal point without cluttering the letterform. The hatch-line texture inside the J does double duty as a design element (texture) and reinforces depth, while the solid orange dot creates emphasis — it's the first thing the eye lands on, precisely because it's the only unbroken shape in the mark. I used a complementary color scheme (blue and orange sit opposite each other on the color wheel) specifically because that relationship creates the strongest possible contrast without needing a third color — it keeps the sticker feeling bold and confident rather than busy.

For balance, the thick, heavy stroke of the J is offset by the small scale of the orange dot — a large shape and a small shape carrying roughly equal visual weight, which is what keeps the mark from feeling lopsided despite being asymmetric. If I revisited this, I'd experiment with pushing the hatch pattern's contrast further, or trying the same mark in an inverted colorway to see whether it holds up as a flexible brand asset, not just a single fixed sticker.
